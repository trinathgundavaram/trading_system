## Why this release exists

A 3-year Stage 1 replay over 60 tickers scored 29,882 candidate-days and
returned **zero** trades. The score distribution had a maximum of 52.94% that
was byte-identical across two runs two days apart.

A distribution whose maximum does not move between runs is a ceiling, not a
signal. The scoring function could not reach its own threshold: three
compounding compressions held the maximum reachable composite at 72.5% while
`rules/dynamic_thresholds.py` went on comparing against a fixed 0–100 bar. The
system was not declining to trade — it was arithmetically incapable of it.

The second half of this release is the same finding one level down. Once trades
flowed, the first result was profit factor 1.10, which read as "the strategy has
no edge." It was also an artifact: `engine/backtest_engine.py` was modelling an
exit policy the system does not use.

## Why this is v3.0.0

`scripts/classify_change.py` reports MAJOR and this ships as MAJOR — the two
agree, which is the uninteresting case.

What is worth recording is that `classify_change.py`'s own docstring uses this
exact change as its worked example:

> *"Deleting `* b.qual_mult` from the weighted sum (§19) is a one-line change
> that a conventional scheme would call a patch. It re-scores every candidate in
> the system, which means pre-change trade history may not be pooled with
> post-change history. That is a major bump."*

That is precisely what happened, arrived at from the opposite direction — a
zero-trades audit rather than a release review.

**`config_fingerprint` is UNCHANGED at `cc9a149613427f56`, and that is a gap,
not a reassurance.** It hashes `config.yaml` values only. Every change here moved
the decision function in *code*, touching no config key, so §17's
self-partitioning mechanism does not fire. Nothing automatically distinguishes a
pattern row scored before this release from one scored after it.

In practice `scripts/tp` rescues this: `tp install` gives v3.0.0 its own
database, so v2.4.0's pattern history physically cannot be pooled with v3.0.0's.
That is a property of the deployment model, not of the fingerprint. Anyone
querying across a shared database — or restoring a v2.4.0 dump into v3.0.0 —
must partition on `app_version` by hand.

## What changed

### Scoring ceiling — `rules/swing_buy_rules.py`

- **The 1.25× redistribution clamp could not guard what it claimed to, and cost
  17pp of ceiling doing nothing** — `swing_buy_rules.score` — §19. The
  2026-07-21 review asked that no bucket "dominate the entire score" when
  several go dark at once. The implementation clamped `scale`, but `scale`
  multiplies every available bucket uniformly and therefore cancels out of the
  share ratio entirely:
  `share_b = (w_b − unavail_b)·scale / (w_avail·scale)`. Relative dominance was
  identical at 1.25 and at 1.54. Replaced with a genuine per-bucket share guard
  (`MAX_BUCKET_SHARE = 0.45`). Because share is scale-invariant, an
  over-concentrated composite cannot be repaired by rescaling — so it surfaces
  as a confidence dock and `data_coverage` telemetry, never as a silently lower
  ceiling.

- **`_qualification_multiplier` was applied twice** — `swing_buy_rules.score` —
  §19. Bucket contribution was `(points/max_points) × weight × qual_mult`, where
  `qual_mult` is itself a function of `points/max_points` — an effective ≈ pct².
  A bucket at 80% completion contributed 0.704; at 60%, 0.420. Real candidates
  sit at 70–85% and essentially never at 100%, making this the dominant
  real-world compressor (mean score 21.7% against a 50% bar). It also was not
  the documented design: that function's docstring says it exists to *replace* a
  binary qualification cliff, not to compose on top of an already-proportional
  term. Now applied once via the anchor curve, recomputed from
  `_effective_bucket_pct` so EXTERNAL's partial-outage handling still feeds it.

- **The threshold never learned the score scale had shrunk** —
  `swing_buy_rules.score` / `rules/dynamic_thresholds.py` — §19. 25% of an
  unavailable bucket's weight is deliberately left dead, but `calc_threshold`
  returns a bar on a fixed 0–100 scale, so the dead weight was charged twice:
  once compressing the score, once leaving the bar where full coverage would
  need it. A nominal 50% bar really demanded 55.9% of measurable evidence, and
  got stricter every time a data source went down — every outage silently
  becoming a regime nobody chose. The threshold is now rescaled by the
  achievable ceiling. `final_score_pct` keeps its existing meaning so stored
  `final_score` rows stay comparable, and the rescale is a no-op at full
  coverage.

This is the same defect class as the VOLATILITY_EXPANSION drag fixed on
2026-07-15 (its 7% weight capped every non-squeeze stock at 93%) — at four times
the magnitude, reached by a different mechanism, and again found only by a
zero-trades audit rather than by a test. Hence the regression tests below.

### Exit model — `engine/backtest_engine.py`

- **The backtest was not replaying the exit policy the system runs** —
  `simulate_forward_exit` — Stage 1.5. It held **one** stop price for a whole
  20-day hold while production runs `engine/stop_state_machine.py`'s 6-state
  ratcheting stop every cycle. Of 302 trades, 213 reached `breakeven_r` (0.5R)
  and **123 of those still recorded a full stop-out**, because nothing ever
  moved the stop up. The 208 losers were not going straight down — 53.8% reached
  +3% first, 17.8% reached +10% first, then round-tripped. Losers that rally
  first and winners that never dip is the signature of a missing trailing stop,
  not a bad entry. Now replays `stop_state_machine.calculate()` bar-by-bar with
  production's `should_advance()` ratchet.

- **Point-in-time discipline in the replay** — `simulate_forward_exit`. The stop
  in force during bar *i* is priced off bar *i−1*'s close. Re-pricing from bar
  *i*'s own close and then testing that bar's low against it would be look-ahead
  that flatters trailing stops *specifically* — it would manufacture the exact
  improvement the change is meant to measure. Pinned by
  `test_stop_is_priced_off_the_previous_bar_not_the_current_one`.

- **R-multiple expectancy** — `summarize` / `_exit_result`. The replay
  equal-weights percentage returns, so a stop-width comparison read on
  `avg_outcome_pct` rewards a wider stop for taking more risk per trade.
  `expectancy_r` holds risk at 1R the way `engine/position_sizing.py` would.

- **Exit vocabulary** — `_exit_result`. Moved to `rules/common.py`'s canonical
  `EXIT_KINDS`. A stop hit in `TREND_FOLLOWING` is a `trailing_stop` protecting
  profit, not a `stop_loss`; collapsing the two made every trailing exit read as
  a failure. `time_based_close` (never an `EXIT_KINDS` member) → `time_stop`.

## What this does NOT change

- **No `config.yaml` value.** Deliberately — see Config changes below.
- **No behaviour at full data coverage.** With all six buckets live,
  redistribution is a no-op (`scale == 1.0`, `achievable_ceiling == 1.0`) and a
  perfect setup still scores ~100 against an unmodified bar. Pinned by
  `test_full_coverage_scoring_is_unchanged_by_the_ceiling_work`.
- **No `engine/stop_state_machine.py` logic.** The backtest now *calls* it; the
  state machine itself is untouched, so live exit behaviour is unchanged.
- **No schema.** No migration.
- **Legacy telemetry keys survive.** `weight_redistribution_scale_uncapped` and
  `redistribution_scale_capped` are retained (the latter pinned `false`) so
  existing UI / `analytics/decision_replay.py` / learning consumers do not
  KeyError on an old signals row.

## Behaviour you will notice

**Many more signals will clear the bar.** This is the point of the release, and
it will look alarming if you are not expecting it. Under degraded data coverage
— which is every Stage 1 backtest cycle, and any live cycle with finviz/FMP down
— scores that previously topped out near 52% now reach into the 70s, and the bar
they are compared against is lower.

Backtest, 6 tickers × 3 years, unchanged ticker set:

| | v2.4.0 | v3.0.0 |
|---|---|---|
| trades | 0 → 302¹ | 396 |
| win rate | 29.8%¹ | 53.5% |
| profit factor | 1.10¹ | 1.23 |
| exits | 208 SL / 71 TP | 181 SL / 155 trailing / 55 TP / 5 time |

¹ v2.4.0 produced 0 trades; the 302-trade column is v3.0.0's scoring fix with
the *old* fixed-stop exit model, isolating the two halves of this release.

New fields on every scored signal: `data_coverage.achievable_ceiling`,
`bucket_shares`, `max_bucket_share`, `bucket_share_exceeded`; and on every
backtest trade: `outcome_r`, `risk_pct`, `exit_stop_state`.

## Config changes

| key | old | new | action required |
|-----|-----|-----|-----------------|
| — | — | — | none |

**No config value changed, deliberately.** The parameter sweep that informed the
recommendations below ran on `engine/ta_fallback.py`, not the `pandas_ta`
backend every threshold in `config.yaml` was derived on —
`engine/ticker_analyzer.py` fails closed on exactly this for exactly this
reason. Tuning against fallback numbers is how a threshold derived on one
backend gets quietly replaced by one derived on another.

Swept as full re-runs (entries are path-dependent — `i = exit_idx + 1` — so
nothing can be re-filtered from a saved trade list), read on `expectancy_r`:

| config | n | win | PF | exp_R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stop=8 (current) | 396 | 53.5% | 1.23 | 0.119 |
| **stop=16** | 330 | 58.8% | 1.48 | **0.202** |
| stop=16, trail=0.75 | 370 | 56.8% | 1.28 | 0.157 |
| stop=16, r=4.0 | 324 | 58.3% | 1.44 | 0.202 |
| stop=16, threshold=62 | 257 | 59.5% | 1.44 | 0.178 |

Only the stop cap matters — it was binding on 94 of 181 stop-outs, clamping
tighter than the ATR justified. Trail and `r_multiple` are at optimum, and
**raising the threshold makes things worse**, the opposite of what the broken
exit model implied (where ≥65 looked like PF 1.60). Acting on that earlier
reading would have degraded the system. Candidate change for a follow-up
release, after re-measurement on `pandas_ta`:
`risk.TURBO.stop_loss_swing_pct: 8 → ~14–16`, and only alongside confirmation
that position sizing scales inversely with stop width.

## Validation

- `pytest tests/` — **384 passed, 99 skipped** (sandbox; 3 further tests in
  `tests/test_phase3_portability.py` require a running Postgres).
- 14 new tests: 5 in `tests/test_scoring_sanity.py` pinning the achievable
  ceiling under every availability combination; 9 in
  `tests/test_backtest_exit_replay.py` covering the initial stop coming from the
  real state machine, the breakeven ratchet, the never-widen rule, same-bar
  tie-breaking, vocabulary legality, and the point-in-time guard.
- Controlled A/B for the scoring fix — identical tickers, window and indicator
  backend, only the change differing: max score 51.18% → 78.89%, mean 25.09% →
  39.37%, trades 2 → 70.

**Validation is INCOMPLETE and this release must not be armed live on it.**

1. Every number above came from `ta_fallback`, not `pandas_ta`. `tp install`
   checks for the reference backend and warns if it is missing — that warning is
   the difference between re-validating and repeating this caveat.
2. On a mega-cap holdout (MSFT, GOOGL, BAC, PFE, CMCSA, F) the picture is
   **profit factor 1.01, expectancy 0.004R** — no edge at all. Median risk 2.66%
   means the 8% cap never binds there, so the stop finding is inapplicable and
   the result is byte-identical either way. The entire measured edge sits in
   high-volatility names over a 2023–2026 window containing a large
   crypto/momentum run. That is one volatility bucket in one favourable period.
3. `learning/walk_forward.py` exists and has never been pointed at this.

Re-validation procedure: `docs/deploy_v3.0.0.md` steps 5 and 8.

## Known issues carried forward

- **`config_fingerprint` cannot detect a code-only decision change.** Recorded
  above; unresolved. §17's mechanism is config-derived by construction, so a
  code-level fingerprint (or hashing the decision-path files) is the real fix.
- **181 stop-outs with median MFE +1.54%** — the entry admits trades that
  immediately go against and never work at any point. That is where the
  remaining upside is, and it is a scoring problem, not an exit one.
- **`THESIS_BROKEN` never fires in the replay** — `exit_score` is passed 0, as
  Stage 1 has no historical sell-side scoring. `avwap_earnings` and
  `recent_swing_low` are likewise absent, so `TRADE_CONFIRMING` never triggers.
  Both leave the stop where other stages put it; neither invents a level.
- **Bid/ask remain synthetic** in Stage 1, so `SPREAD_WIDE` and the
  execution-quality spread term are non-discriminating. Unchanged from v2.4.0.
- **Position sizing is not modelled** in the replay; every trade is
  equal-weighted by percentage return. `expectancy_r` is the workaround, not a
  fix.
